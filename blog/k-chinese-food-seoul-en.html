Where to Try K-Chinese (Korean-Chinese) Food in Seoul: Jjajangmyeon, Jjamppong and Tangsuyuk at Taetae

K-Chinese, or Korean-Chinese food, is Korea's own take on Chinese cooking: black bean noodles (jjajangmyeon), spicy seafood noodle soup (jjamppong) and sweet and sour pork (tangsuyuk). You can try all three at Taetae (태태), a Korean-Chinese restaurant in Oksu-dong, Seoul, one stop from Hannam Station, where every dish is cooked to order.

Written by the Taetae team · Updated 2026-11-26 · Prices as of Nov 2026

What is K-Chinese food?

It is the Chinese-rooted cooking that grew up in Korea over more than a century and became everyday Korean food.

Koreans call it junggukjip (중국집) food. The dishes carry Chinese names, but the flavors, the way people order and the way they share are Korean. Most Koreans have memories tied to it: jjajangmyeon on moving day, tangsuyuk for a family celebration, jjamppong after a long night.

What should I order on my first visit?

Pick your noodles, then share a plate of tangsuyuk for the table.

FAQ

Is K-Chinese the same as Chinese food?
It has Chinese roots, but it developed in Korea and is Korea's own take, with its own dishes like jjajangmyeon, jjamppong and tangsuyuk.
What is the most popular K-Chinese dish?
Jjajangmyeon (black bean noodles), usually shared with tangsuyuk (sweet and sour pork).
Is there vegan K-Chinese food at Taetae?
Yes. Taetae Jjajang (₩10,000) and Fish-Fragrant Eggplant (₩23,000) are vegan.
